Offshore accounting works well or it works badly, and the difference is usually visible in the first conversation. These are the questions worth asking, including the ones that are awkward for us to answer.

## 1. Who exactly will be doing the work?

You want names and qualifications, not a headcount. "A team of five" can mean five qualified accountants or one qualified reviewer and four people doing data entry. Ask which.

## 2. What hours do they work?

Overlap matters more than total hours. Four hours of genuine overlap with your working day is worth more than a full shift that finishes before you start, because questions get answered the same day rather than the next one.

## 3. What happens when someone leaves?

Everyone leaves eventually. The right answer involves documented processes and a named backup who already has context, not a promise that turnover is low.

## 4. Who is my point of contact, and will it change?

The person who sells you the engagement is often not the person who runs it. Ask whether the contact you meet on the first call stays with the account.

## 5. What is the close date, and what happens if you miss it?

A firm that will not commit to a date is telling you something. Ask what has happened historically when a close slipped and what changed as a result.

## 6. How is my data accessed and protected?

Specifically: who has access, at what permission level, whether data is downloaded to local machines, whether access is logged, and whether individual staff sign NDAs rather than just the firm.

## 7. What is explicitly out of scope?

Most disputes come from an unstated assumption. Offshore accounting firms generally do not file US tax returns or provide attest services; those stay with your CPA. Get the boundary written down.

## 8. How do you handle a backlog?

If your books are behind, catching up is a different job from staying current, with different pricing. Ask for the clean-up scoped and priced separately so you can see what each part costs.

## 9. Can I talk to a client with a business like mine?

The most useful question and the one people skip. A firm that has done this well will have someone willing to spend fifteen minutes on the phone.

## What good answers sound like

Good answers are specific and include limits. "We close by the fifth business day for clients under 800 transactions a month, and by the eighth above that" is a better answer than "we always close on time." A firm that never mentions a constraint has either not thought about it or is not telling you.
